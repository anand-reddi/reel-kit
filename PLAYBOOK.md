# Hook & share playbook (@techinsixty)

Every reel has four jobs: **stop the scroll, hold to the end, get shared, earn the follow.**
The main metric is shares (DMs and story reposts), not likes or comments. Make the viewer feel smart enough to send
it to a friend ("bro, how did you not know this?").

## 1. Hook: first 1–2 s (pick one type per reel)
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Curiosity gap: result first, how later | "This free app replaces 5 apps on your PC." |
| Call out a pain | "Still paying for ___? Stop." |
| Specific number | "9 MB. That's the whole app." |
| Visual pattern break | crash zoom, the panel dripping open, a number slamming in: motion in frame 1, never a calm title card |
| "Did you know" | "Your Windows PC can do this and nobody told you." |

### The hook is a system, not one line
Plan these together for every hook option (from Hooksmith, MIT):
- **Spoken hook** and **on-screen text** do different jobs. The text is the *what* (the claim, the number, the
  scenario, 3–5 words in frame 1, readable on mute, clear of the top and bottom fifth); the voice is the *why/how*
  (the mechanism or the twist). If both say the same thing, rewrite one.
- **First spoken word is the most interesting word.** Cut everything before it ("So basically", "Today I want to",
  "Let me show you", unless the demo starts in the same frame).
- **Open loop + withheld answer:** name the one question the viewer now needs answered, and what stays hidden until
  the payoff. Close every loop you open.
- **Rehook between 3 and 10 s**, where attention dips: a contradiction ("But here's the weird part…"), a missing
  condition, a scene switch, a reveal, a crossed-out line. It must connect to the open loop, not be random surprise.
- **Payoff** big enough for the setup; if it isn't, shrink the promise.

### Hook anti-patterns (rewrite on sight)
- Fake proof: invented views, users, stars, "I tested this", timelines. Only facts we checked.
- Viewer attack ("Your PC is trash"). Pull in through the shared pain instead ("Most PCs ship with this turned off").
- Hedged questions ("Have you ever wondered…?"). Questions only when they carry a specific claim.
- Stranger pronouns ("He built this in a weekend") about someone the viewer hasn't met; name the role instead.
- Vague superlatives (best, ultimate, game-changing, insane) with no number, name or outcome beside them.
- Series metadata first ("Day 4 of…", "Part 2"): it goes in a small label, never the first spoken words.
- One-word caps punch stacks as the *hook* ("STOP. USING. CHROME."). Kinetic slams are fine as punctuation later;
  the opening text has to carry information.
- No real viewer: if the hook fits anyone, it holds no one. Add the niche, the situation or the constraint.

### Score every hook before showing it (50 points)
| Category | Points |
|---|---:|
| First-second clarity | 8 |
| Open loop strength | 10 |
| Viewer specificity | 7 |
| Stakes and urgency | 7 |
| Rehook and retention path | 8 |
| Visual/text hook stack | 5 |
| Payoff integrity | 5 |

0–20 weak, 21–34 usable, 35–43 strong, 44–50 breakout-ready. Only show hooks at 35+; rewrite the rest first.
The three hook options must use different jobs (pain call-out, number, belief break, open loop, demo bridge), not
three wordings of one idea.

## 2. Hold: retention to the end
- Spine: **hook → escalation → payoff → CTA.** Join beats with "but" / "so" / "therefore", never "and then":
  a beat joined by "and then" usually isn't earning its place.
- Value tease ladder: hook gap → partial answer → deeper question → example → rehook → mechanism → payoff. Never
  hook → full answer → generic tips.
- Open loop ("…and the last one is the best") that is actually paid off at the end.
- Put the second-strongest moment near the top (to survive the early drop) and the strongest at the payoff.
- Something new every 2–3 s: a cut, card, zoom or reveal on the beat.
- Dual track: the voice says one thing while the screen shows a complementary thing (the proof, the UI, the number).
- Keep it to 20–30 s. Loop endings (last frame flows into the first) earn rewatches.
- Check every draft against the **three failure modes** and name the exact line where attention drops:
  1. Weak hook (gone by 0:01): setup, brand-first, vague. Lead with the payoff or the stake.
  2. No escalation (bleeds out): it flatlines. Add the open loop earlier, cut beats that don't raise stakes, add a
     rehook where it sags.
  3. Buried payoff (stalls mid): compress the setup, tease the payoff in the hook, move a taste of it earlier.

## 3. Share: the main goal
- A "send this to…" moment: name the friend who needs it ("…your friend who still uses Electron apps"). A named
  persona earns sends; "share with a friend" doesn't. The share intent should start forming in the hook, so the hook
  already hints at who needs this (from Vyral's Reels playbook, MIT).
- Shapes that get sent: the favour (info one specific friend needs), the receipt (settles an argument), the callout
  ("your friend who has 80 Chrome tabs open"), a list with a repeatable name, the permission slip ("you're not the only
  one who…").
- The send line gets its own beat after the payoff, in voice and on screen, before the Follow line.
- Make the viewer look smart: a little-known trick, a free tool, a hidden setting.
- Lead with **free / open-source / works today**: these get shared the most.
- One clean poster frame near the end (logo + one line) that works as a story repost.

## 4. Save
- Lists and steps ("3 free Windows tools", "set it up in 30 s") get saved, and saves push reach.
- End on the name or link, clearly visible, so the save is useful later.

## 5. Follow
- Series framing: "Tool #12 of 100", "Hidden Windows apps, part 3".
- The same face and look every time (the presenter and the paper/dark-red style).
- A follow line with a reason: "Follow, I post one free tool like this every day."

## 6. Comments (only without begging)
- A mild debate: "Better than Rainmeter?" / "Would you switch?"
- A keyword offer: "Comment WIN and I'll DM you the link."

## Before making any video: show the options first
Do not script or render until the owner has picked. Present, for this specific topic:
1. **2–3 hook options** (from section 1, written out for this topic), each as a full system: spoken hook, on-screen
   text, open loop, withheld answer, rehook (3–10 s), payoff, and its score out of 50. Recommend one.
2. **The share angle**: who they would send it to, and the line that makes them.
3. **The save / follow angle**: series name, list or steps, follow line.
4. **Format and length**: reel (9:16 voice + presenter) or motion film (16:9, no voice); target seconds.
5. Optional: an ending (loop, poster frame, keyword CTA).

Discuss, settle the choices, then write the script and build. Before the frames board, read the script once as a
stranger: mark the exact line where you'd swipe, name the failure mode, fix it.

## Standing defaults (the user's choices)
- **Intro sound on every video:** the Iron Man repulsor (`assets/sounds/intro-repulsor.wav`) plays at 0 s and the first
  words wait 0.4 s for it. It is on by default; `"intro": false` turns it off, `"intro": {"sound", "gain", "lead"}` swaps it.
- **Two cuts of each reel:** the Instagram cut ends "Follow for more like this." (`follow` card); the YouTube cut is the
  same script ending "Subscribe for more like this." (`follow` card with `"platform": "youtube"`). The link line says
  "link in bio" on Instagram and "link on my channel" on YouTube.
- **No double character on the ending:** the Follow / Subscribe card already shows the avatar, so `reel.py` hides the
  presenter on `follow` and `profile` scenes (layout forced to `none`). `"presenter": true` on the scene keeps him.
- **Natural speech:** write lines the way a person talks: contractions ("it's", "you'll"), short connectors ("So he...",
  "Now...", "The catch?"), one idea per line, numbers said the way people say them ("24 gig", "up to 44 percent").
  Avoid written phrasing like "utilises" or long noun stacks. The Backburner reel (Nov 2026) is the reference.
